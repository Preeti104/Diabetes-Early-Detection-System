How GlucoCheck works

A closer look at the model, the data behind it, and what a result does and doesn't tell you.

The model

GlucoCheck is powered by a gradient-boosted decision tree model trained to recognise patterns associated with Type 2 diabetes across clinical, physiological and lifestyle measurements. Before selecting this model, several candidate algorithms were trained and compared on the same data, and the strongest performer on held-out patient records was chosen for the live tool.

The data

The model was trained on a dataset of 20,000 patient records covering fourteen features: demographic details (gender, age), clinical measurements (glucose, blood pressure, skin thickness, insulin, BMI, cholesterol, diabetes pedigree function), and lifestyle and history factors (physical activity, smoking status, alcohol intake, family history, hypertension).

How a screening works

When you submit the form on the Prediction page, your inputs are validated, transformed into the same numerical format the model was trained on, and passed through the model in real time. The result is a probability — how closely the entered profile matches patterns seen in diabetic patients in the training data — shown alongside a plain-language label.

What a result is — and isn't

A GlucoCheck result is a statistical screening signal, not a medical diagnosis. It's designed to support — not replace — a conversation with a qualified clinician and standard diagnostic testing. Results should always be interpreted alongside professional medical judgement.

Privacy

Screenings are stateless: patient details you enter are used only to generate the result on screen and are not stored, logged, or shared afterwards.
